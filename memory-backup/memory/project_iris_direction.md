Scott's biggest concern is building a fancy dashboard that never actually helps them make better financial decisions.

**The real goal of Iris:**
- Get finances balanced and diversified
- Lower taxes through smart moves (ISO exercises, tax-loss harvesting, backdoor Roth)
- Know when to sell stale positions and where to rotate money
- Get specific buy/sell/hold recommendations based on THEIR portfolio
- Track market trends filtered to their holdings, not generic news
- Model scenarios: "what if I cut monthly investing from $4k to $2k?"

**What's built (presentation layer):**
- Dashboard with collapsible sections, stat cards, insights
- Budget tracking with import from BofA/Citi/Capital One
- Ask Iris chat connected to Gemini with full portfolio context
- Health scores, sector allocation, equity tracking

**What's NOT built yet (the actual value):**
- Proactive investment recommendations ("sell X, buy Y, here's why")
- Market intelligence filtered to their holdings
- Rebalancing suggestions with tax impact modeling
- Scenario modeling for contribution changes
- Position age tracking ("you've held SOXX for 2 years, consider rotating")
- Monthly investment optimization (current ~$4-6k/mo across shares + BTC)

**Why:** Scott explicitly said "my biggest fear is we stop building and have a fancy beast that doesn't achieve the goal." The dashboard is the interface; the intelligence engine is the product.

**How to apply:** Always prioritize features that drive real financial decisions over UI polish. The next big build should be the proactive recommendation engine, not more dashboard sections.

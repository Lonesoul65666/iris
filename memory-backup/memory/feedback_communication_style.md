Don't overwhelm with data or alerts. Scott explicitly said he'll ignore briefings if they're too long or frequent. Same applies to Claude's own responses — keep them short. Use a decision table or 1-2-3 options when he needs to pick a path, but default to 1-2 sentences. No walls of text for simple issues.

**In-product copy & UI tone:** Laid-back, informal, conversational. Iris is a "fun" application — finances suck, so make them feel less heavy. Casual phrasing ("blame the budget", "let's go", "nothing's locked in") beats clinical wording. Reserve serious/clinical tone ONLY for major alerts or critical issues where the user genuinely needs to pay attention. Default vibe = friendly assistant, not a banking app form.

**Why:** Alert fatigue is real for him. He doesn't have time to parse walls of financial data. He needs the noise filtered down to what actually matters.

**How to apply:**
- Default views should be calm and focused, not 47 charts
- Daily digest should be ONE notification, most days under 30 seconds to read
- "Nothing to report" is a valid and good output — earns trust by not crying wolf
- Depth available on click-through, not forced on the surface
- Every piece of jargon gets a plain English explanation inline
- Dark premium aesthetic (glassmorphism, gradients) — not Robinhood toy, not Bloomberg terminal

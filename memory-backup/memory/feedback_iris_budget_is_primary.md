**Status note (2026-05-02):** Still operative. Phase 1 is exclusively the Budget Engine per `docs/adr/0001-phase-1-scope.md`. The competitor list has widened — Honeydue and Zeta now sit alongside Monarch / YNAB / Mint / Copilot as comparison apps, especially for the couples-fun positioning. The "blame the budget" framing is also now central to the new mission ("the only argument left is what color the sheets should be").

---

The entire original purpose of Iris was to get Scott and Claire's spending under control — "one place to rule them all," "one source of truth," so they can **blame the budget** instead of each other. Investment tracking was added second.

When evaluating Iris or recommending priorities: budget and spending are **co-equal** with (or more important than) investment intelligence. Iris must be competitive with Mint / YNAB / Copilot / Monarch as a budget app, not just a portfolio dashboard.

A budget that "works, is fun, and is attainable" is the north star. If the budget side fails, the whole project fails by Scott's own definition. Framing Iris as "investment-first, budget secondary" is a miscalibration I made on 2026-04-20 and got corrected for directly.

**Why:** Scott and Claire want financial harmony. The budget is the referee. Iris is how they consent to a referee in advance so money fights become "the budget says..." instead of "you spent..." It's a relationship tool as much as a finance tool.

**The trigger:** Scott was a paying Monarch user, got tired of the subscription, started Iris to replace it. Monarch is the specific benchmark he's trying to match — not YNAB or Mint abstractly. Investment tracking began as scope creep that stuck because it made sense. Treat Monarch-parity for budget as the **primary success condition**.

**Reference apps to audit against (in Scott's stated priority order):** Monarch, YNAB, Mint, Copilot. If Iris is missing "awesome stuff" from ANY of the four, at least consider it — don't silently skip.

**How to apply:**
- Never recommend de-emphasizing budget features.
- Rank budget-related work equal to or higher than investment features when prioritizing.
- Frame Iris externally as "budget + investment intelligence" or "money operating system," not "investment intelligence platform with some budget stuff."
- When a feature could go in either direction, favor the budget angle if it's less developed than the investment angle.
- Investment features should *feed back* into budget (e.g., "you're $400 over budget — here's what to pull from the DCA" — cross-domain, not siloed).
- "Fun" and "attainable" matter. This is not YNAB-style rigor. Budget copy/tone must feel supportive, not scolding.
- When asked to evaluate or prioritize, explicitly compare against Monarch first (the one he actually used and paid for).
